## The Big Idea

STAIRS computes a **score** for every potential target on a given night. The score combines: how good the target is for your telescope (size, brightness), how good the sky conditions are (light pollution, moon, weather), and practical scheduling constraints (rise/set times, meridian flips). The highest-scoring targets become your imaging plan.

## The Details

### Two-Tier Scoring

STAIRS uses a two-tier scoring system:

**1. Object Suitability Score (OSS)** — property of the target alone (0–100):

- FOV fit: how well the target fits on your sensor (40% weight)
- Surface brightness: how concentrated the target's light is (30% weight)
- Altitude: how high the target gets (30% weight)

**2. Absolute Quality Score (AQS)** — OSS modified by tonight's conditions:

- OSS × Sky Quality Score (SQS) multiplier
- SQS accounts for: weather, moon, target altitude throughout the night
- Bortle multiplier (light pollution) further scales the score

### The Pipeline

```
Target Catalog
     ↓
  Compute OSS for each target
  (FOV fit + SB + altitude)
     ↓
  Fetch weather forecast
  (cloud, seeing, humidity, wind)
     ↓
  Compute SQS
  (weather × moon × target altitude trace)
     ↓
  Compute AQS = OSS × SQS × Bortle_mult
     ↓
  Sort targets by AQS
     ↓
  Build observing plan
  (schedule top targets in available windows)
     ↓
  Present ranked plan to user
```

## In STAIRS

This pipeline runs every time you request a plan. The entire architecture is designed to give you a **ranked list of targets + a schedule** so you can go outside knowing exactly what to image and when.
