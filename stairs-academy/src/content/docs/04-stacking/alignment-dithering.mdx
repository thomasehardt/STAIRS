## The Big Idea

Even with good tracking, stars drift between frames. Alignment software detects star positions in each frame and shifts/rotates them to match a reference. **Dithering** deliberately moves the telescope between frames to make this alignment even more effective at removing noise.

---

## The Details

### How Alignment Works

1. **Star detection**: the algorithm finds bright spots (stars) in the image
2. **Pattern matching**: it identifies the same stars in the reference frame
3. **Transform calculation**: it computes how much the new frame has shifted, rotated, or scaled relative to the reference
4. **Warping**: the frame is shifted/rotated to match the reference (sub-pixel precision)

### Plate Solving

A more sophisticated version is **plate solving** — matching the star pattern against a catalog to determine exactly where the telescope is pointing. This gives absolute coordinates rather than just relative alignment.

### Dithering

Dithering is intentionally moving the telescope a few pixels between frames or every few frames. This:

- Spreads hot pixels and dead pixels across different locations in each frame
- Turns fixed pattern noise into random noise (which stacking can reject)
- Improves the final image's smoothness

Without dithering, a hot pixel at position (100, 200) appears at (100, 200) in every frame — stacking won't remove it. With dithering, that hot pixel falls on different sky positions, so the stack can reject it.

### Smart Telescopes and Dithering

Many smart telescopes **don't dither** (or have limited dithering capability) because:

- Dithering adds time between frames (settle time)
- The short exposure / large stack approach naturally handles hot pixels via sigma clipping
- The mount's precision limits how small a dither can be reliably executed

---

## In STAIRS

Dithering affects the **practical SNR** improvement rate. For smart telescopes without dithering, the effective $\sqrt{n}$ improvement is slightly degraded by residual fixed pattern noise. STAIRS can optionally account for this in its integration time calculations.
