## The Big Idea

Three sensor parameters determine how good your images will be: **Quantum Efficiency** (how many photons become electrons), **Read Noise** (the electronic background hum), and **Gain** (how finely you measure the charge). Understanding these helps you choose optimal exposure settings.

---

## The Details

### Quantum Efficiency (QE)

QE is the percentage of photons that successfully create a detectable electron. It varies with wavelength:

- Most silicon sensors peak in the near-infrared (600–800nm)
- Typical QE at peak: 60–80% for modern back-illuminated CMOS
- QE drops off sharply below 400nm (UV) and above 1000nm (IR)

**Back-illuminated (BSI) sensors** rearrange the silicon layers so light hits the photodiode directly instead of passing through metal circuitry first. This can boost QE by 30–50%.

### Read Noise

Read noise is the uncertainty introduced when reading the charge from each pixel. It's measured in electrons (e⁻). Lower is better.

Typical values:

- Ancient CCDs: 10–20 e⁻
- Modern CMOS: 1–5 e⁻
- Best-in-class: < 1 e⁻

Read noise sets the floor for your signal. If your target delivers only 2 e⁻ per pixel per second, and your read noise is 3 e⁻, a single 10-second frame has SNR ≈ 0.6 — essentially noise. You need to stack many frames to overcome this.

### Gain (Conversion Gain)

Gain determines how many electrons equal one ADU (Analog-to-Digital Unit):

$$g = \frac{\text{electrons}}{\text{ADU}}$$

- **Low gain** (high e⁻/ADU): more headroom, better dynamic range, coarser quantization
- **High gain** (low e⁻/ADU): more sensitivity, less headroom, finer quantization

Most astrophotography cameras have a "unity gain" setting where 1 e⁻ = 1 ADU, which is a good balance.

### Swamping the Read Noise

The goal is to expose long enough that **sky noise** dominates **read noise**. The ratio is:

$$\text{Swamp Ratio} = \frac{S_{\text{sky}} \cdot t}{\sigma_r^2}$$

Where $S_{\text{sky}}$ = sky signal (e⁻/pixel/s), $t$ = exposure time (s), $\sigma_r$ = read noise (e⁻).

A ratio of 10× is the standard target — sky noise should be 10× larger than read noise.

The optimal sub-exposure time is:

$$t_{\text{sub}} = \frac{10 \cdot \sigma_r^2}{S_{\text{sky}}}$$

For a typical smart telescope under Bortle 5 skies: $\sigma_r = 3\text{e}^-$, $S_{\text{sky}} \approx 5\text{e}^-/\text{pixel/s}$:

$$t_{\text{sub}} = \frac{10 \cdot 9}{5} = 18 \text{ seconds}$$

This nicely matches the 10–20s limit imposed by Alt-Az tracking.
