## The Big Idea

The sensor is a grid of millions of tiny buckets (pixels). During an exposure, photons fill these buckets as electrons. When the exposure ends, an Analog-to-Digital Converter (ADC) measures how full each bucket is and assigns a number — that's your digital image.

## The Details

### The Photoelectric Effect

When a photon hits the silicon in a pixel, it transfers its energy to an electron, knocking it loose. This electron is trapped in a **potential well** — a tiny region of the silicon that holds charge. The longer the shutter is open, the more electrons accumulate.

### Key Sensor Parameters

- **Full Well Capacity**: maximum electrons a pixel can hold (10k–100k e⁻). When full, the pixel saturates (pure white).
- **Quantum Efficiency (QE)**: the percentage of photons that successfully generate an electron. Modern CMOS sensors achieve 50–80%.
- **Read Noise**: the electronic noise added when reading the charge. Typically 1–5 e⁻ for modern sensors.
- **Gain/ISO**: how many digital units each electron represents. Low gain = more headroom. High gain = more sensitivity.

### The ADC Readout

After the exposure:

1. The charge in each pixel is shifted to a readout register
2. The charge is converted to a voltage
3. The ADC converts the voltage to a digital number (ADU — Analog-to-Digital Unit)
4. The number is stored as a pixel value in the image file

For a 14-bit ADC, pixel values range from 0 to 16,383.

---

## In STAIRS

Sensor parameters drive exposure planning. STAIRS uses:

- **Pixel pitch** (µm) to compute pixel scale
- **Sensor dimensions** to compute field of view
- **Read noise** in the sub-exposure optimization formula
- **QE** in light collection rate estimates

### Gain and Conversion

The gain converts electrons to ADU:

$$\text{ADU} = \frac{\text{electrons}}{g} + \text{offset}$$

Where $g$ is the gain in e⁻/ADU. A gain of 1.0 means each electron = 1 ADU. A gain of 0.5 means 2 electrons = 1 ADU (more headroom but coarser quantization).

### Full Well and Dynamic Range

The sensor's **dynamic range** is the ratio of the maximum signal to the noise floor:

$$DR = \frac{S_{\text{max}}}{N_{\text{read}}}$$

In stops: $DR_{\text{stops}} = \log_2(DR)$

For a sensor with 50,000 e⁻ full well and 3 e⁻ read noise: $DR = 50,000 / 3 \approx 14$ stops.

### The Photon Transfer Curve

Plotting noise vs. signal on a log-log scale reveals three regimes:

1. **Read noise dominated** (low signal): flat line
2. **Shot noise dominated** (mid signal): slope = 0.5 (Poisson statistics)
3. **PRNU dominated** (high signal): slope = 1.0 (pixel non-uniformity)
