## The Big Idea

Every pixel in a camera sensor is colorblind — it only measures intensity. To capture color, a **Bayer filter** places red, green, and blue filters over individual pixels in a specific pattern. The camera then uses math (demosaicing/debayering) to reconstruct full color at every pixel.

## The Details

### The Bayer Pattern

The standard Bayer array uses a 2×2 repeating pattern:

```
R  G
G  B
```

Green gets twice as many pixels because the human eye is most sensitive to green light. This means:

- 50% of pixels capture green
- 25% capture red
- 25% capture blue

### Demosaicing (Debayering)

The camera firmware interpolates between neighboring pixels to estimate the missing color channels at each pixel. For example, a pixel with a red filter needs its green and blue values estimated from surrounding green and blue pixels.

There are many algorithms, from simple bilinear interpolation to sophisticated AI-based methods. Better algorithms produce sharper images with fewer artifacts.

### Monochrome vs Color

Some astrophotographers use **monochrome cameras** (no Bayer filter) with external filter wheels. This gives:

- Higher resolution (every pixel captures all light)
- Higher sensitivity (no filter absorption losses)
- Ability to use narrowband filters more efficiently
- But requires multiple exposures through different filters

Smart telescopes use color sensors for convenience — you get a color image from a single session.

---

## In STAIRS

Color sensing matters for **surface brightness calculations** — the Bayer filter absorbs ~50-70% of incoming photons (depending on target color). STAIRS accounts for this in its integration time estimates.

### Quantum Efficiency Loss from Bayer

A typical Bayer filter passes:

- ~95% of green light
- ~90% of red light
- ~80% of blue light
- ~0% of light outside these bands

The **effective QE** of a color sensor is approximately:

$$QE_{\text{eff}} \approx QE_{\text{mono}} \times 0.25 \times (T_R + 2T_G + T_B)$$

Where $T$ is the transmission of each filter. Typical effective QE: 25–35% of the monochrome QE.

### Why Green Has Two Pixels

The Bayer pattern doubles green pixels because:

$$V_{\text{luminance}} = 0.299 R + 0.587 G + 0.114 B$$

The human visual system derives most of its spatial information from the green channel. Doubling green pixels improves perceived sharpness.

### Narrowband Imaging with a Color Sensor

With a dual-band filter (Hα + OIII), the color sensor captures:

- Hα (red) in the R pixels
- OIII (blue-green) in the G and B pixels

The result is a false-color image where Hα appears red and OIII appears cyan/teal — the classic "HOO" palette that works well with smart telescopes.
